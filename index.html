Situated Inference: Adapting Frozen Generative Speech Enhancement under Acoustic Distribution Shift

ICLR 2027 submission — under review

Anonymous authors

Diffusion and flow matching models restore noisy speech through iterative conditional generation, while a fixed inference strategy may become unsuitable under the acoustic distribution shifts encountered in real-world deployment. We find that these shifts change the effectiveness and portability of inference strategies even when the enhancer remains frozen. This finding motivates Situated Inference, which specializes the execution of a frozen generative enhancer to a target acoustic distribution using representative paired target speech. Situated Inference estimates a performance profile over a structured inference space whose strategies jointly control observation processing, the reverse starting point, and the solver step count. It uses this profile to specialize the enhancer under either a quality or efficiency objective and fixes the resulting strategy for subsequent inputs. Across four generative backbones and three acoustic conditions, Situated Inference improves perceptual speech quality under the quality objective and reduces the number of function evaluations (NFE) under the efficiency objective. Strategy portability and component analyses further indicate that effective strategies depend on acoustic conditions and that joint adaptation captures benefits beyond changing the solver step count alone.
